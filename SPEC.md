# Ma no Iro 間の色: specification

Version 0.4.1. The palette values are locked for this version; the role map may still gain rows.

`palette.json` is the source of truth. It is generated by `tools/derive.py`, and so are the palette tables in this document, the table in `README.md`, and the data inside `docs/showcase.html`. Nothing in a port should contain a colour that cannot be traced back to `palette.json`.

The words *must*, *should* and *may* are used in their usual specification sense.

## 1. What Ma no Iro is

Ma no Iro (Ma for short) is a two-flavor colour palette for code editors, terminals and interfaces.

| Flavor | Name | Ground | Home colour | Seed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Night | 黒紫 Kuromurasaki | black | purple | green |
| Day | 白緑 Byakuroku | white | green | purple |

The name 間の色 means *the colour of the interval*. 間 is read *ma* in Japanese and *gian* in Vietnamese: the gap between things that gives them their shape. In Ma no Iro the interval is literal. It is the grey where every pair of colours in the palette meets.

## 2. The idea

Ma no Iro is a palette and a short guide to colour. Every explanation it gives has to hold up, so each is stated with its limits. The same rule applies to this section: where a claim is a model, a correspondence or a design choice rather than a measurement, it says so.

**Every colour comes with the colour it takes away.** Fire, stars and screens make colour by giving light off. Almost everything else makes colour by taking light away, and what reaches the eye leans toward the complement of what was taken. Real objects remove a range of wavelengths, not one colour, so the leaning is approximate; the pairs in Ma no Iro are exact because they are solved in a colour model.

Ma no Iro is four of those exchanges:

| Scene | What happens | Pair | Anchor | Limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The leaf | Chlorophyll absorbs strongly at the blue and red ends and less in the middle; the two ends together look purple | green and purple | 555 nm, the eye's daylight peak | Leaves absorb most green too; they look green because they absorb it least |
| The sky | Air scatters short wavelengths in proportion to λ⁻⁴; the scattered light is the blue sky and the direct sun looks yellower | blue and yellow | the λ⁻⁴ curve | Sunlight is uneven and the eye is weak in violet, which is why the sky is blue, not violet |
| The water | Vibrations of the water molecule absorb red; over metres, water and ice show cyan and blue | cyan and red | 700 nm, the end of red | Real lakes also depend on what is dissolved in them and what they reflect |
| The ink | Soot absorbs almost every wavelength; paper and snow scatter them all back | black and white | none of the light, and all of it | No ink is perfectly black and no paper perfectly white |

**The anchors.** 555 nm is the peak of the CIE photopic luminosity function, the eye's sensitivity to brightness in daylight. 700 nm is the end of red: in the CIE 1931 standard observer, every longer visible wavelength has the same chromaticity. The λ⁻⁴ curve is the exact shape of Rayleigh scattering and also the limit a blackbody's spectrum approaches as its temperature rises without end.

**The eye.** The retina has three cone types, most sensitive near 570, 545 and 445 nm (Stockman and Sharpe fundamentals). Colour is how strongly light excites the three, so different spectra can look identical. Purple is non-spectral: no single wavelength excites the long and short cones while sparing the medium, so purple is made by the eye. Post-receptoral comparisons run roughly between green and magenta and between violet and yellow; they do not line up exactly with the perceptual opposites red–green and blue–yellow.

**The interval.** A screen's colours fill the RGB cube, whose eight corners are the terminal's eight colours. Opposite corners are complements for the screen's primaries, and the four lines joining them cross at mid grey. That grey is 間, the interval, and it is Ma no Iro's ground. Seen down the grey axis, the cube is a hexagon of six colours around a grey centre: the project's mark. The cube is a model of a display, not of light or perception.

**Night and day.** In the sRGB luminance formula, green contributes 71.52% of white and red and blue together 28.48%. Pairing green with the day flavor and purple with the night is a design choice grounded in that split.

**Traditions.** Classical Chinese colour theory named five 正色, cardinal colours, and 間色, intermediary colours, between them; in one common list green, purple and jade (綠, 紫, 碧) are intermediaries. Yin and yang, opposites that each hold a seed of the other, give the flavors their seed. In Japanese aesthetics 間 is the interval or negative space that gives a form its shape, the idea that a thing can be known by what it lacks. These are correspondences and borrowed images, not proofs, and classical colour words do not map one to one onto modern hues; nothing in the derivation depends on them.

## 3. Derivation

1. **Anchors.** Green: the spectral colour at 555 nm. Red: the spectral colour at 700 nm. Blue: the chromaticity of the λ⁻⁴ spectrum. All three are computed from the CIE 1931 standard observer (`tools/cie1931.py`).
2. **Complements.** Purple, cyan and yellow are the colours on the far side of the D65 white point from green, red and blue.
3. **Solving.** Every accent in the palette, at its lightness and chroma, is the colour whose CIE xy chromaticity lies on the ray from D65 toward its anchor, or directly away from it for a complement. Hues therefore vary slightly between rungs of the same family (for example purple from 320.7 to 323.4), because OKLCH hue and xy direction are not perfectly aligned.
4. **Lightness and chroma.** Per role, these are the design parameters in `tools/derive.py`.
5. **Derived colours.** ANSI brights, washes and alpha equivalents follow fixed rules.

The anchors are reference points chosen for each scene, not measurements of it; only λ⁻⁴ is the physics of its own scene. Once they are set, no hue in Ma no Iro is chosen by eye. Moving the green anchor moves every green and every purple; the other anchors are fixed by physics.

| Green anchor | Dark keyword purple hue |
| --- | --- |
| 540 nm | 332.5 |
| 550 nm | 326.5 |
| **555 nm** | **321.8** |
| 557.7 nm (aurora oxygen line) | 318.4 |
| 565 nm | 303.6 |

## 4. Rules

**R1. Two flavors, four pairs.** Ma no Iro has exactly two flavors. They share the same four pairs and the same role map.

**R2. The seed.** Each flavor carries the other half's colour as a seed, as the dot in a yin-yang does, and uses it only for the point of attention: the cursor, the current search match and the focus ring. Night: green. Day: purple. This is also the highest-visibility choice, because green is the most luminous hue on black and the darker purple stands out most on white.

**R3. The home colour.** Each flavor's own accent marks the chrome: the active tab indicator, the active line number, matching brackets, badges, primary buttons and the status bar mode. Night: purple (`keyword`). Day: green (`function`).

**R4. Code is written in the leaf, the sky and the water.** Keywords are purple and functions are green (the leaf). Strings are blue (the sky). Types are cyan (the water). Numbers, constants and escapes are a pale purple. Red and yellow never colour code: red is kept for errors and deletions, yellow for warnings and search. Meaning is fixed across flavors. The scenes explain where each colour comes from; the assignment to tokens is a design choice, made so that the most frequent tokens carry the palette's own pair.

**R5. The ground is the interval.** Neutrals are pure grey, chroma 0, in both flavors. Selection, current line and word highlights are grey steps of the ground, so the code under them never changes hue. Neither flavor uses pure black or pure white as its base.

**R6. One green in code.** Green colours only functions, so an added line in a diff is the only green background a reader sees.

**R7. The dose.** Chroma falls as area grows. Grounds and highlights are grey, washes are faint, syntax is moderate, and only the seed is vivid. A port must not raise the chroma of any colour.

**R8. Two yellows on white.** A yellow that is readable as text on white does not exist, since dark yellow is olive or brown. The day flavor has `yellow` for signals at 3:1 and `yellow_text` for text at 4.5:1. In the night flavor both names hold the same colour. Ports use `yellow` for squiggles, icons, borders and the search wash, and `yellow_text` for anything read as text, including ANSI yellow.

**R9. Flat colour.** No gradients, anywhere.

**R10. Opaque first.** Every colour is specified opaque. Where a target requires translucent highlights (VS Code and Zed do for several backgrounds, so decorations underneath stay visible), a port must use the `alpha.hex8` value from `palette.json`, which composites over `base` to the exact opaque colour.

| Element | Token |

The 16 ANSI colours are listed per flavor in `palette.json` under `ansi`, and every one of them belongs to a pair: black and white to the ink, green and magenta to the leaf, blue and yellow to the sky, cyan and red to the water. ANSI green is `function` and ANSI magenta is `keyword`. Bright colours step one rung toward the text colour (lightness +0.08 on the night flavor, −0.08 on the day), enough that a bright reads as its own step. On the night flavor bright green is also held to chroma 0.11, so it stays softer than the seed. The seed is never an ANSI colour: programs print ANSI colours freely, and the cursor must stay the one point of attention (R2). On the day flavor, bright yellow is the `yellow` signal, and ANSI white is `dim` with `overlay` as its bright, so text printed in white stays faintly visible on a white ground.

### 6.8 Rainbow levels

Bracket pairs, indent guides and similar nesting cues cycle through four levels, one per exchange: `keyword`, `string`, `type`, `function`.

### 6.9 Fallbacks

- A token the target does not recognise is `text`.
- A target without italics drops the style; it never substitutes a colour.
- A target that cannot draw a border for the current search match uses a `seed` background with `base` text.
- A target limited to 16 colours uses the ANSI map.
- A target that requires translucent highlights uses the `alpha.hex8` values.

## 7. Accessibility

The policy is enforced by `tools/derive.py --check`, which must pass before any release.

- **Resting text:** every text role must reach 4.5:1 on `base`, `surface` and `hl_low`. `text`, `subtle` and `muted` must also reach 4.5:1 on `mantle`.
- **Overlays:** every syntax colour must reach 3:1 on `hl_med`, `hl_high` and every derived wash.
- **Signals:** `yellow` and `seed` must reach 3:1 on `base`.
- **The seed stands alone:** every one of the 16 ANSI colours must be at least 0.07 from `seed` in Oklab.
- **Brights are a step:** every ANSI bright must be at least 0.07 from its normal colour in Oklab.
- **The dose (R7):** no colour may have as much chroma as `seed`.
- **Selection is a step:** `hl_high` must differ from `overlay` and `hl_med` by at least 0.04 in OKLCH lightness.
- **On accent:** `base` text must reach 4.5:1 on every accent used as a background.
- **Distinct in normal vision:** every pair of syntax colours, `text` and `muted` must be at least 0.07 apart in Oklab.
- **The leaf:** `keyword` and `function` must stay at least 0.10 apart in Oklab under normal vision and simulated protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia (Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes 2009, full severity).

Weaker pairs under simulated colour-vision deficiency are reported by the script and documented rather than blocking. In this version there are four, all at ΔE 0.033 or more and all involving `type`, `blue` or `cyan`. Dark cyan cannot hold much chroma in sRGB, so to a protanope it sits close to grey, and the day flavor's types are separated from comments and punctuation by lightness instead. Comments are italic and diagnostics carry icons, so the closest pair, day-flavor types against comments for protanopes, also has a non-colour cue.

## 8. Port requirements

A port is accepted when it meets all of the following.

- **Generated, not typed.** Colours come from `palette.json` through a template. A port must not contain a hex value that is not in `palette.json`.
- **Both flavors.** Every port ships Kuromurasaki and Byakuroku.
- **Complete role coverage.** Every role in section 6 that the target supports is mapped. Anything unmapped is listed in the port's README.
- **Samples.** The port is checked against the files in `samples/` and ships a screenshot of each flavor using `samples/palette.ts`.
- **Files.** Each port has a `README.md` (what it is, a screenshot of each flavor) and install instructions that a new user can follow without guessing.
- **Names.** Flavors are displayed as `Ma no Iro Kuromurasaki` and `Ma no Iro Byakuroku`, and identified as `ma-no-iro-kuromurasaki` and `ma-no-iro-byakuroku` where a target needs an id. The repository, packages and site use the slug `ma-no-iro`.

## 9. Naming and voice

- **Ma no Iro** (間の色): the project, *the colour of the interval*. Read ま の いろ. Use the full name on first mention, in titles and in package names; `Ma` is the short form in running text.
- **Kuromurasaki** (黒紫): the night flavor, black-purple. Read くろむらさき. The other reading of 黒紫, *kokushi*, collides with 黒子 (a mole on the skin), so it is not used.
- **Byakuroku** (白緑): the day flavor, white-green. Read びゃくろく. It is also the name of a pale green pigment in Japanese painting, made by grinding malachite until it lightens.
- **The scenes** are the leaf, the sky, the water and the ink, plus the eye, the interval and the traditions. Use them when explaining why a colour is where it is, and always keep the limits that go with them.
- **Token names** are by role (`keyword`, `hl_high`), not by colour, so a port is a lookup.

When writing about Ma no Iro, lead with the exchange, then the number, then the limit. Never present a model as nature, a correspondence as a proof, or a design choice as a law. Say "purple", "green", "blue", "yellow", "cyan" and "red"; avoid "violet", "lavender", "lime" and "teal", which name other hues. Ma no Iro is not a purple theme: it is four pairs around a grey.

## 10. Decision log

- **0.1: one pair.** Green at 557.7 nm, then 555 nm, and its complementary purple, with red, yellow, blue and cyan hand-set as conventions. Syntax used only the pair, which left the night flavor mostly purple and put green on strings, functions, selection and added lines at once.
- **0.2: four pairs.** Every colour is now one half of a complementary pair, and all eight are the terminal's eight. Red and cyan are anchored at 700 nm, blue and yellow at λ⁻⁴; no hue is picked by eye.
- **Selection is grey.** Highlights moved from the home colour to grey steps of the ground so that code under them keeps its colour and diffs have only one green.
- **The ink, not the night.** The fourth scene was the night; night is dark because no light arrives, which is not an exchange. Ink and paper absorb and scatter, so the scene is now the ink. The flavors are still night and day.
- **Every scene states its limits.** Each explanation carries a strictly-speaking note, so the story is poetic and correct at once.
- **Home colour is chrome.** The yin-yang flip lives in the chrome and the seed; syntax is identical in both flavors.
- **The standard observer table, not a fit.** The official 1 nm CIE 1931 table replaced an analytic approximation in 0.1.
- **Two flavors, not four.** Catppuccin's three dark flavors sit at OKLCH lightness 0.24, 0.28 and 0.33. Ma keeps one night and one day.
- **Name: Ma → Ma no Iro.** Searchable, and it says what it means.
- **0.3: the seed stands alone.** Bright green (night) and bright magenta (day) were the seed, so programs printed the cursor colour everywhere. They now step one rung like the other brights, and `derive.py` fails if any ANSI colour lies within ΔE 0.07 of the seed.
- **0.3: selection is a clear step.** `hl_high` was 0.03 lightness from the border grey on the night flavor and 0.01 on the day. It moved to 0.34 and 0.86, still pure grey, and `derive.py` requires 0.04 from `overlay` and `hl_med`.
- **0.3: anchors are reference points.** The README said each pair had a physical anchor, which read as if the green was measured from a leaf. 555 nm and 700 nm are properties of the eye and the observer, chosen for the leaf and the water; only λ⁻⁴ belongs to its own scene.
- **0.3: lead with both flavors.** The night flavor's chrome is purple, so a night-only first image reads as a purple theme. READMEs, banners and port pages show both flavors, or the hexagon, first.
- **0.4: types apart from comments.** On the day flavor, `type` was ΔE 0.009 from `muted` for protanopes, effectively the same colour. Dark cyan is capped near chroma 0.076 by the sRGB gamut, so chroma could not separate them; `type` moved to L 0.45 and `subtle` to 0.38, which raised the pair to 0.039 and cleared two other weak pairs.
- **0.4: only the seed is vivid.** Night `red` had chroma 0.20, the same as the seed, which broke R7. It moved to 0.17, and `derive.py` now fails if any colour reaches the seed's chroma.
- **0.4: brights are a visible step.** Most brights were ΔE 0.06 from their normals. The step grew from 0.06 to 0.08 lightness, day-flavor ANSI white moved from `overlay` to `dim`, and `derive.py` requires 0.07.
- **0.4.1: no anchor slider.** The showcase had a slider that moved the green anchor and re-solved the purple. It made a fixed palette look adjustable and the one anchor that comes from the eye look arbitrary, so it was removed. The table above keeps the comparison for readers who want it. Anchoring the leaf at its purple (c555 nm) was tried and dropped for the same reason: 555 nm is a measured peak of vision, and naming the pair by its complement added nothing.
